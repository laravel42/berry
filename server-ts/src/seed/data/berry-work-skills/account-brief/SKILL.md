# Account Brief

## When to use

A person plans outreach, a meeting or a proposal for a named organisation.

Not for: Collecting personal data about individuals beyond their public professional role.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Sales Specialist.

## Gather first

- The account name and website.
- Any history in the workspace (tasks, notes, threads).
- What is being offered.

## Deliverable

Produce an account brief: what the organisation does, recent public news with dates and links, likely needs the offer meets, people and roles involved (public professional information only), history with us, and open questions.

## Steps

1. Collect public sources: the organisation's site, reports, news, filings.
2. Summarise what they do and what changed recently.
3. Map likely needs to what the offer actually does.
4. List known contacts with role and public source; nothing private.
5. Write what you know, what you assume and what to ask.

## Quality checks

- Every fact has a dated source.
- Only professional, public information about people is included.
- The fit to the offer is honest, including where it does not fit.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The account is a competitor, a government body with procurement rules, or in a sanctioned market.

## Avoid

- Do not scrape or guess personal emails or phone numbers.
- Do not overstate the fit.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person could walk into a call with this brief and sound prepared.
