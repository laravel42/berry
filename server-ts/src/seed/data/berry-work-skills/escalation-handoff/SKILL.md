# Escalation Handoff

## When to use

A case hits an escalation trigger: refund, exception, legal threat, vulnerable customer, repeated failure.

Not for: Cases that the help content answers.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Customer Support Specialist.

## Gather first

- The full case history.
- Relevant policies.
- What has been tried.

## Deliverable

Produce a handoff note: who the customer is (only what is needed), what they want, what happened in order, what was tried, the relevant policy, options with their consequences, and urgency.

## Steps

1. Summarise the case in three sentences.
2. Build a short timeline.
3. State what the customer asked for in their words.
4. List options under policy, and what each would cost or risk.
5. Say how urgent it is and why.

## Quality checks

- The note fits on one screen.
- Options are within policy or marked as exceptions.
- Tone about the customer is neutral.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The customer may be at risk of harm or is vulnerable: mark the handoff urgent and say why in its first line.

## Avoid

- Do not decide the outcome.
- Do not editorialise about the customer.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can decide in minutes.
