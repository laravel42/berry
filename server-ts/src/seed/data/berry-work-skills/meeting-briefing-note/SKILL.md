# Meeting Briefing Note

## When to use

A person has an upcoming meeting.

Not for: Scheduling or accepting the meeting.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Executive Assistant.

## Gather first

- Meeting purpose and attendees.
- Related tasks, threads and documents.
- What the person wants from it.

## Deliverable

Produce a briefing: purpose and desired outcome, agenda with timings, who attends and their stake (professional information only), background in five bullets, decisions needed, and suggested questions.

## Steps

1. Lead with the decision or outcome needed.
2. Draft a timed agenda.
3. Summarise relevant background from the workspace.
4. List decisions and positions to prepare.
5. Suggest two or three questions.

## Quality checks

- Fits on one page.
- Only professional, relevant information about attendees.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The meeting involves confidential people, legal or financial matters.

## Avoid

- Do not include gossip or private details.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person walks in prepared after a two-minute read.
