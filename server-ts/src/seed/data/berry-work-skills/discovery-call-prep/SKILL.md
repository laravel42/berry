# Discovery Call Prep

## When to use

A call with a prospect or client is scheduled.

Not for: Running the call.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Sales Specialist.

## Gather first

- Account brief.
- Who attends and their roles.
- What the person wants out of the call.

## Deliverable

Produce a one-page call brief: goal of the call, agenda, five to eight open questions, likely objections with honest answers, what not to promise, and the ideal next step.

## Steps

1. State the outcome that would make the call a success.
2. Write open questions about their situation, not our product.
3. List likely objections and an honest answer to each.
4. Note facts the person must not commit to on the call.
5. Propose the next step to ask for.

## Quality checks

- Questions are open, not leading.
- Answers to objections are true and sourced.
- The brief fits on one page.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The call may involve pricing, contract or legal terms not yet approved.

## Avoid

- Do not script a pitch; prepare a conversation.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person goes into the call knowing what to ask and what to avoid.
