# Offboarding Checklist

## When to use

A departure has been decided and confirmed by a person.

Not for: The decision, the terms or the conversation about leaving.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the People Partner.

## Gather first

- Leaving date and role.
- Systems, equipment and responsibilities held.
- What may be shared about the departure.

## Deliverable

Produce an offboarding checklist: handover plan, access removal timeline, equipment return, final admin to confirm with payroll, knowledge capture, exit survey, and communication plan, each with an owner.

## Steps

1. List responsibilities and plan handovers.
2. Schedule access removal for the right moment.
3. List equipment and how it is returned.
4. Note admin a person must confirm (final pay, benefits).
5. Plan a respectful farewell and communication.

## Quality checks

- Every item has an owner and date.
- Access removal is timed correctly.
- Communication respects what the person agreed to share.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The departure is contested, disciplinary or involves legal terms.

## Avoid

- Do not speculate about reasons for leaving.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person leaves well and nothing is left behind.
