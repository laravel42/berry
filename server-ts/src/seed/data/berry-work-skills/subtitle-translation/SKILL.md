# Subtitle Translation

## When to use

A video, webinar or course needs subtitles in another language.

Not for: Voice-over recording.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Translator.

## Gather first

- The source transcript or subtitle file with timings.
- Target language.
- Glossary.

## Deliverable

Produce a subtitle file with timings preserved, lines within reading-speed limits, notes where meaning was condensed, and speaker labels where needed.

## Steps

1. Keep timecodes unchanged.
2. Translate meaning, condensing to fit reading speed.
3. Keep lines short and break at natural points.
4. Mark condensed passages in notes.
5. Read through against the timings.

## Quality checks

- Timecodes are unchanged.
- Line length and reading speed are within limits.
- Names and terms follow the glossary.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The content is medical, legal or safety training.

## Avoid

- Do not drop meaning to fit; condense and note.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The subtitles read comfortably and match the speech.
