# Contract Summary

## When to use

A contract has been received, is about to be signed or is being renewed.

Not for: Legal advice or saying it is safe to sign.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Legal Assistant.

## Gather first

- The full document, including schedules and amendments.
- Which side we are on.

## Deliverable

Produce a summary table: parties, start and end, renewal and notice, price and payment terms, key obligations each side, liability and indemnity, termination rights, governing law, with the clause number and quoted wording for each; plus questions for a lawyer.

## Steps

1. Confirm you have the whole document and all amendments.
2. Extract each item with its clause number and quote.
3. Note the governing law and jurisdiction.
4. Highlight dates that need action.
5. End with questions a lawyer should answer.

## Quality checks

- Every line quotes the clause it relies on.
- Missing schedules are noted.
- The summary is labelled as not legal advice.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A decision depends on how the law applies.
- The document is about to be signed.

## Avoid

- Do not paraphrase in a way that changes meaning.
- Do not cite a clause you did not read.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader knows what the contract commits them to and what to ask a lawyer.
