# Source Evaluation

## When to use

Research uses web pages, reports, studies or news of mixed quality.

Not for: Rejecting sources only because they disagree.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The sources found.
- The claims taken from each.

## Deliverable

Produce a source table: source, date, author or publisher, type (primary, secondary, opinion), method if any, conflicts of interest, reliability rating with reason, and the claims it supports.

## Steps

1. Record date, author and publisher for each source.
2. Classify as primary, secondary or opinion.
3. Check method and sample where claims are numeric.
4. Note funding and conflicts of interest.
5. Rate reliability and say why.

## Quality checks

- Every source was opened and read.
- Dates are recorded.
- Numeric claims are traced to their origin.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Key claims rest on a single unreliable source.

## Avoid

- Do not cite a source you did not open.
- Do not treat repetition as confirmation.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader knows which sources to trust and why.
