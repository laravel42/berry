# Plan vs Actual Variance Explanation

## When to use

A month, quarter or project has ended and actuals are available.

Not for: Fixing the books.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Financial Analyst.

## Gather first

- Budget or forecast.
- Actuals for the same period and basis.

## Deliverable

Produce a variance table (line, plan, actual, difference, percent), explanations for material variances with evidence, whether each is timing or permanent, and the effect on the full-year outlook.

## Steps

1. Confirm both sides use the same period, currency and basis.
2. Compute differences and pick a materiality threshold.
3. Explain each material variance with evidence (invoices, volumes, prices).
4. Classify as timing or permanent.
5. Update the outlook for permanent ones.

## Quality checks

- Differences are computed correctly and signs are consistent.
- Every explanation has evidence or is marked unexplained.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A variance suggests error, loss or fraud.

## Avoid

- Do not explain a variance with a story you cannot evidence.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader knows why the numbers moved and what it means for the year.
