# Course Blueprint

## When to use

Outcomes are agreed and the training needs a structure.

Not for: Writing every lesson in full.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Learning Designer.

## Gather first

- Learning outcomes.
- Time available, format (in person, online, self-paced) and group size.

## Deliverable

Produce a blueprint: modules in order, sessions per module with duration, the outcome each serves, activities and practice, assessments, materials needed, and prerequisites.

## Steps

1. Order outcomes from foundation to application.
2. Group them into modules.
3. Plan sessions with explanation, practice and check.
4. Place assessments where they test outcomes.
5. List materials and who makes them.

## Quality checks

- Every outcome is taught and assessed.
- Time adds up.
- Practice time is at least as long as explanation time.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The course leads to a certificate or qualification.

## Avoid

- Do not fill sessions with lecture alone.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A trainer could deliver the course from the blueprint plus materials.
