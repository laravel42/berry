# Brand Voice Guide

## When to use

Materials sound different depending on who wrote them, or a new writer or agent is starting.

Not for: Logo, colour or visual identity.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Marketing Strategist.

## Gather first

- The best existing materials, chosen by a person.
- Audience profile and positioning.
- Words and phrases people like and dislike.

## Deliverable

Produce a voice guide: three to five voice traits each with "this, not that" examples, vocabulary to use and avoid, how the voice shifts by situation (sales, support, bad news), and before/after rewrites.

## Steps

1. Collect strong and weak examples from the workspace.
2. Name three to five traits and show each with a do and a don't.
3. List preferred and banned words.
4. Show how tone flexes for good news, bad news and support.
5. Rewrite two real passages to show the guide in use.

## Quality checks

- Every trait has an example from real material.
- The guide is short enough to read in five minutes.
- It agrees with the house style guide, or notes conflicts.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The voice change would alter brand commitments already made publicly.

## Avoid

- Do not describe a voice only with adjectives ("friendly, bold").

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Two writers using the guide produce text that sounds like one organisation.
