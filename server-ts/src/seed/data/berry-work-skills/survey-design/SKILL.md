# Survey Design

## When to use

A decision needs data that can be gathered by asking people.

Not for: Sending the survey or collecting personal data without a stated purpose.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The decision it informs.
- Who will be asked and how.
- Any privacy constraints.

## Deliverable

Produce a survey: purpose statement, consent and privacy text, questions in order with answer options, which question answers which sub-question, estimated time, and an analysis plan.

## Steps

1. Map each sub-question to one or two survey questions.
2. Write neutral, single-topic questions.
3. Use balanced answer scales and an "other" or "prefer not to say" option.
4. Keep it under about ten minutes.
5. Write the analysis plan before collecting.

## Quality checks

- No leading or double-barrelled questions.
- Personal data collected is the minimum needed.
- Every question maps to a decision.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The survey asks about health, children or other sensitive topics.

## Avoid

- Do not ask questions you will not use.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The survey can be sent and its results analysed as planned.
