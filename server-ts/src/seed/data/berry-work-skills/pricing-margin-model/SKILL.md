# Pricing and Margin Model

## When to use

A new offer, a price change or a quote needs cost and margin checking.

Not for: Setting the price.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Financial Analyst.

## Gather first

- Unit costs and overheads.
- Proposed prices or options.
- Expected volumes.

## Deliverable

Produce a model: cost per unit, margin at each price, break-even volume, sensitivity to cost and volume changes, and comparison with competitor prices where public.

## Steps

1. Build unit cost from its parts with sources.
2. Compute margin and break-even for each price option.
3. Test sensitivity to the biggest uncertainties.
4. Add public competitor prices with dates.
5. Summarise trade-offs.

## Quality checks

- Costs trace to sources.
- Margin and break-even are calculated correctly.
- Taxes and fees are handled consistently.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Prices are below cost or would breach a contract.

## Avoid

- Do not hide overheads to make margins look better.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can set the price knowing its margin and break-even.
