# Audience Definition

## When to use

A goal, product or offer exists but nobody has written down who it is for, what they care about and where they can be reached.

Not for: Formal user research with interviews or surveys; hand that to the Research Analyst.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Marketing Strategist.

## Gather first

- The business goal and the offer (product, service, event, cause).
- Anything known about current customers, members, donors or users.
- Past campaign results or feedback, if any.

## Deliverable

Produce an audience profile: one to three segments, each with who they are, what they need, what stops them, where they spend attention, the evidence behind each line and what is still a guess.

## Steps

1. Restate the goal in one sentence and the behaviour the audience must change.
2. List candidate segments, then keep the one to three that matter most to the goal.
3. For each segment write needs, objections, triggers and channels.
4. Tag every statement as evidence (with its source) or assumption.
5. Name the riskiest assumption and the cheapest way to check it.

## Quality checks

- Each segment can be told apart from the others.
- No segment is defined by a protected trait unless the offer genuinely requires it.
- Assumptions are labelled, not mixed in with evidence.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The audience choice would exclude or target a group in a way that could be discriminatory.
- The goal itself is unclear or contradicts another goal in the workspace.

## Avoid

- Do not invent personas with made-up quotes or numbers.
- Do not describe "everyone" as the audience.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A writer could pick up the profile and know who they are writing for without a meeting.
