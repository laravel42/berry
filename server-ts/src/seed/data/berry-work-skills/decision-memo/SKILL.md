# Decision Memo

## When to use

A decision is pending and the information is scattered.

Not for: Making the decision.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Executive Assistant.

## Gather first

- The question to decide and the deadline.
- Options and their facts.
- Views of the people involved.

## Deliverable

Produce a one-page memo: the decision needed and by when, background in three lines, two to four options with pros, cons, cost and risk, people's positions, a recommendation if asked, and what happens next for each option.

## Steps

1. State the decision in one sentence with its deadline.
2. Summarise background briefly.
3. Lay out options fairly.
4. Record positions of stakeholders.
5. Add a recommendation only if the person asked for one.

## Quality checks

- Options are presented fairly.
- Facts are sourced.
- Fits on one page.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The decision is urgent and the person is unreachable.

## Avoid

- Do not stack the options to favour one.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person can decide after one read.
