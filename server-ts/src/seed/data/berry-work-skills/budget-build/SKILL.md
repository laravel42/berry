# Budget Build

## When to use

A period or project needs a spending and income plan.

Not for: Approving spend.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Financial Analyst.

## Gather first

- Past actuals, if any.
- Known commitments (salaries, rent, contracts).
- Goals and planned activities.

## Deliverable

Produce a budget table by month or phase: income and cost lines, totals, the assumption behind every line, a contingency line, and a short summary of the biggest drivers.

## Steps

1. Set currency, period and whether figures include tax.
2. Start from commitments, then recurring costs, then planned activities.
3. Write the assumption next to every line.
4. Add contingency and say why that size.
5. Check every total and subtotal.

## Quality checks

- Totals reconcile to line items.
- Every line has an assumption or a source.
- Currency, period and tax basis are stated.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The budget shows a shortfall that affects payroll, debts or legal obligations.

## Avoid

- Do not hide assumptions in formulas.
- Do not present a guess as a quote.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A decision-maker can challenge any line by reading its assumption.
