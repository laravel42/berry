# Option Cost Comparison

## When to use

A person is choosing between two or more options with money attached.

Not for: Making the decision.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Financial Analyst.

## Gather first

- The options and their quotes or terms.
- Time horizon.
- What matters besides cost.

## Deliverable

Produce a comparison: total cost over the horizon, cash timing, risks and how they could change the cost, non-financial factors, a sensitivity check on the key assumption, and which option wins under which conditions.

## Steps

1. Put every option on the same horizon and basis.
2. Include one-off, recurring, exit and hidden costs.
3. Show cash timing, not only totals.
4. Test the result against the most uncertain assumption.
5. State which option wins and under what conditions.

## Quality checks

- Options are compared like for like.
- Every cost cites its quote or assumption.
- The sensitivity check is shown.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- An option involves debt, a long contract or tax consequences.

## Avoid

- Do not ignore exit or switching costs.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can decide and knows what would change the answer.
