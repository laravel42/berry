# Lesson Plan

## When to use

A specific lesson is coming up.

Not for: Grading real learners.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Learning Designer.

## Gather first

- Outcome for the lesson.
- Learners' level and number.
- Duration and setting.

## Deliverable

Produce a lesson plan: outcome, materials, timed sequence (hook, explanation, guided practice, independent practice, check, close), differentiation for different levels, and a quick check of understanding.

## Steps

1. Open with a hook that connects to learners' experience.
2. Explain in small steps with an example.
3. Do guided practice, then independent practice.
4. Check understanding with a quick question or task.
5. Close with a summary and what comes next.

## Quality checks

- Timings add up to the slot.
- There is a check of understanding before the end.
- Support and stretch are provided.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Learners are minors or vulnerable and the activity carries risk.

## Avoid

- Do not plan more content than the time allows.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Someone else could teach the lesson from the plan.
