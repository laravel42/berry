# Interview Panel Briefing

## When to use

A panel is about to interview.

Not for: Training on employment law.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Recruiter.

## Gather first

- Job description, scorecard and interview plan.
- Panel members and stages.

## Deliverable

Produce a one-page briefing per panel member: the role in brief, the criteria they assess, their questions, how to take notes and score independently, and questions never to ask.

## Steps

1. Summarise the role and outcomes.
2. Assign criteria and questions to each member.
3. Explain note-taking against criteria.
4. List prohibited questions and topics.
5. Explain the debrief process.

## Quality checks

- Each member knows exactly what they assess.
- Prohibited topics are listed.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A panel member asks to add questions on protected topics.

## Avoid

- Do not share other interviewers' scores before the debrief.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Every interviewer runs a consistent, fair interview.
