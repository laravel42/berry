# Call Notes to Next Steps

## When to use

Notes, a transcript or a thread from a customer conversation exist.

Not for: Internal meetings without a customer; the Operations Coordinator handles those.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Sales Specialist.

## Gather first

- Notes or transcript.
- Who attended.
- The deal or relationship context.

## Deliverable

Produce a summary (needs, concerns, decisions), a follow-up email draft for the customer, and next steps as tasks with owners and dates.

## Steps

1. Pull out what the customer said they need and their concerns, in their words.
2. List what was agreed and what is still open.
3. Draft a short follow-up that confirms understanding and next steps.
4. Create tasks with owners and dates; mark any without an owner.
5. Note risks to the deal or relationship.

## Quality checks

- The summary does not add commitments that were not made.
- Every next step has an owner or a flag.
- The follow-up matches what was actually said.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A commitment was made on the call that needs approval.

## Avoid

- Do not paraphrase the customer into saying more than they did.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Nothing from the call depends on someone's memory.
