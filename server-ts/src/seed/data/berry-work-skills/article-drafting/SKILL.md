# Article Drafting

## When to use

There is a brief or a clear request for a long-form piece with a known reader.

Not for: Short social posts (Social Media Manager) or editing someone else's draft (Editor).

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Content Writer.

## Gather first

- The brief: reader, purpose, length, tone, deadline.
- Source material and any style guide.
- Examples of pieces the person likes.

## Deliverable

Produce a draft as a file on the task with two or three headline options, a one-line summary, the body with subheadings, and a list of sources used.

## Steps

1. Write one sentence: who reads this and what they do afterwards.
2. Outline: the point first, then the three to five sections that support it.
3. Draft in plain words, short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph.
4. Check every fact, figure and quote against its source.
5. Cut ten to twenty percent; then write headline options.

## Quality checks

- The main point is in the first two paragraphs.
- Length is within the brief.
- Every quote and statistic appears in the source list.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The brief and the sources disagree on a fact.
- The piece would make a claim about health, money or law.

## Avoid

- Do not invent quotes, statistics or sources.
- Do not pad to reach a word count.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The draft matches the brief and an editor could start on it.
