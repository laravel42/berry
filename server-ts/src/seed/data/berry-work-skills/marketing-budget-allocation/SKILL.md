# Marketing Budget Allocation

## When to use

A budget amount is known or assumed and must be divided before a campaign or period.

Not for: Approving or spending the money.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Marketing Strategist.

## Gather first

- Total budget and period.
- Goals and success measures.
- Past cost and results per channel, if any.

## Deliverable

Produce an allocation table: channel or activity, amount, share of total, expected result and cost per result with its basis, a test reserve, and the rule for moving money mid-period.

## Steps

1. Fix the total, period and currency.
2. Fund commitments and proven channels first, with evidence.
3. Hold a reserve (often ten to twenty percent) for tests.
4. Estimate cost per result from data or mark it as a guess.
5. Write when and how money moves between lines.

## Quality checks

- Lines add up to the total.
- Every expected result states its basis.
- The reserve and reallocation rule are explicit.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The allocation exceeds the approved budget or commits to contracts.

## Avoid

- Do not spread money evenly to avoid choosing.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can approve the split and knows how it will be adjusted.
