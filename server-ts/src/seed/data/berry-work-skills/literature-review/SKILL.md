# Literature Review

## When to use

A decision, proposal, course or paper needs the state of evidence on a topic.

Not for: Medical or legal advice to individuals.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The question and inclusion criteria.
- Databases or sources available.
- Time box.

## Deliverable

Produce a review: search method (terms, sources, dates), inclusion criteria, a table of studies (design, sample, findings, limitations), synthesis by theme, strength of evidence, gaps, and references.

## Steps

1. Define inclusion and exclusion criteria.
2. Record searches so they can be repeated.
3. Screen and tabulate included studies.
4. Synthesise by theme and weigh quality.
5. State the strength of evidence and gaps.

## Quality checks

- Every study was read, not only its title.
- Search method is reproducible.
- Limitations are reported.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Conclusions would inform health, safety or legal decisions.

## Avoid

- Do not count studies as votes regardless of quality.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader knows what the evidence says and how strong it is.
