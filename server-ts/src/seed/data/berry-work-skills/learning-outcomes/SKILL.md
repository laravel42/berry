# Learning Outcomes

## When to use

Someone wants to teach something and has not said what learners must be able to do afterwards.

Not for: Course content (see course-blueprint).

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Learning Designer.

## Gather first

- Topic and the reason for the training.
- Who the learners are and what they already know.

## Deliverable

Produce three to seven outcomes, each starting with an observable verb, the condition and standard, and how each will be checked.

## Steps

1. Ask what learners must do differently after training.
2. Write outcomes with observable verbs (explain, calculate, carry out), not "understand".
3. Add conditions and standards where useful.
4. Pair each outcome with how it will be checked.
5. Remove outcomes that are nice but not needed.

## Quality checks

- Every outcome is observable.
- Each has a way to check it.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Outcomes involve safety, health or a regulated qualification.

## Avoid

- Do not write "understand" or "be aware of" as outcomes.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A trainer and a learner agree on what success looks like.
