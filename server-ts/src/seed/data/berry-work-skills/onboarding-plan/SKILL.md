# Onboarding Plan

## When to use

Someone is joining and has a start date.

Not for: Contracts and payroll setup; a person handles those.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the People Partner.

## Gather first

- Role, team, manager and start date.
- Tools, accounts and equipment needed.
- Key people to meet and things to learn.

## Deliverable

Produce an onboarding plan: before day one, week one day by day, weeks two to four, first ninety days goals, who owns each item, and a checklist the new person can tick.

## Steps

1. List what must be ready before day one and who prepares it.
2. Plan week one day by day: welcome, access, people, first small win.
3. Set thirty, sixty, ninety day goals with the manager.
4. Name an owner for each item.
5. Create tasks for preparation items.

## Quality checks

- Day one has a named person welcoming them.
- Every item has an owner.
- The first week includes a real, small piece of work.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Accessibility or health adjustments are needed (handle privately with a person).

## Avoid

- Do not overload week one with reading.
- Do not share the new person's personal details beyond what is needed.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The new person and manager both know what happens every day of week one.
