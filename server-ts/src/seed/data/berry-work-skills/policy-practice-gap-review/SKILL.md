# Policy and Practice Gap Review

## When to use

Policies are old, people are confused, or complaints suggest inconsistency.

Not for: Investigating an individual.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the People Partner.

## Gather first

- Current policies.
- Evidence of practice: tasks, questions asked, anonymised examples.

## Deliverable

Produce a gap table: policy clause, observed practice with evidence, the risk of the gap, and options (change policy, change practice, clarify), plus outdated policies to retire.

## Steps

1. List each policy and its last review date.
2. For each clause, note evidence of practice.
3. Classify gaps by risk to people and the organisation.
4. Offer options with trade-offs.
5. List policies past their review date.

## Quality checks

- Evidence is anonymised.
- Each gap has a risk level and options.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A gap suggests a legal breach or unfair treatment.

## Avoid

- Do not name individuals as examples.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can decide which gaps to close first.
