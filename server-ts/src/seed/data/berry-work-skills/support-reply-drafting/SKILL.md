# Support Reply Drafting

## When to use

A customer message is on a task or shared by a person.

Not for: Sending replies or promising refunds, credits or dates.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Customer Support Specialist.

## Gather first

- The customer message and history.
- Help articles and policies in the workspace.
- Account details a person shares (only what the case needs).

## Deliverable

Produce a reply draft that answers the question asked first, then the likely next question, quotes or links the policy relied on, and a note to the sender on anything to check.

## Steps

1. Identify the actual question and the customer's emotion.
2. Find the help article or policy that answers it; quote it.
3. Answer first, then explain, then next steps.
4. Acknowledge frustration without blaming anyone.
5. If no policy answers it, say so in the note and file the gap.

## Quality checks

- The reply answers what was asked.
- Every policy statement has a source in the workspace.
- No promise of refunds, credits or dates.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The customer asks for a refund, exception or compensation.
- The customer threatens legal action, mentions harm or is vulnerable.
- The issue is a product bug (to the Product Lead).

## Avoid

- Do not ask for passwords or full card numbers.
- Do not answer from memory when the policy is not in the workspace.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can send the reply with at most a light edit.
