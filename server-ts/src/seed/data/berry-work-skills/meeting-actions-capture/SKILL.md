# Meeting Actions Capture

## When to use

A meeting happened and notes or a transcript exist.

Not for: Customer calls (see call-notes-to-next-steps).

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Operations Coordinator.

## Gather first

- Notes or transcript.
- Attendees.

## Deliverable

Produce a meeting record: decisions made, actions as tasks with owner and due date, open questions with who will answer, and a short summary for people who missed it.

## Steps

1. Separate decisions, actions, discussion and open questions.
2. Write each action as a verb with an owner and a date.
3. Mark actions without an agreed owner or date as needing one.
4. Create tasks for the actions.
5. Write a three-line summary.

## Quality checks

- Nothing is recorded as decided that was only discussed.
- Owners are people who agreed in the meeting.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Notes record a decision on money, contracts or people that needs formal approval.

## Avoid

- Do not assign actions to people who were not there or did not agree.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Everyone knows what was decided and what they owe.
