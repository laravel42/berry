# Voice-Matched Drafting

## When to use

A person asks for a draft reply or message.

Not for: Sending, accepting or declining on their behalf.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Executive Assistant.

## Gather first

- What the message must achieve.
- Examples of the person's writing.
- Facts and dates to include.

## Deliverable

Produce a draft in the person's voice, short enough to send as is, with any facts to check highlighted.

## Steps

1. Read examples to note length, greeting, sign-off and tone.
2. Write the point first.
3. Keep to the person's usual length.
4. Highlight facts, dates and commitments for checking.
5. Offer one alternative if the tone is uncertain.

## Quality checks

- The draft does not commit the person to anything new without a highlight.
- Facts match the workspace.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The message commits the person to a decision, meeting or money.

## Avoid

- Do not send; the person does.
- Do not imitate the person to anyone without their knowledge.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person sends it with a glance.
