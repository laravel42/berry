# Market Sizing Estimate

## When to use

A plan, pitch or grant needs an estimate of how many people or how much money.

Not for: Precise forecasts.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The market definition.
- Available data points.

## Deliverable

Produce an estimate with both top-down and bottom-up calculations where possible, every assumption and source listed, a range rather than a point, and what would most change the answer.

## Steps

1. Define who or what is being counted.
2. Build a bottom-up estimate from units and rates.
3. Build a top-down estimate from published totals.
4. Compare them and explain the gap.
5. Give a range and the most sensitive assumption.

## Quality checks

- Calculations are shown and add up.
- Each input cites a source or is labelled an assumption.
- The result is a range.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The estimate will be shown to investors or funders as fact.

## Avoid

- Do not present an estimate as a fact.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader can check every step and adjust the assumptions.
