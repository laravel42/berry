# Competitor and Alternatives Landscape

## When to use

Planning, positioning, fundraising or pricing needs context.

Not for: Copying competitors' material.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The market or need.
- Known competitors or alternatives.

## Deliverable

Produce a landscape: list of alternatives (including doing nothing), a comparison table on the dimensions that matter to the audience, pricing where public, recent moves with dates, and implications.

## Steps

1. Define the need, then list everything that meets it.
2. Pick dimensions that matter to the audience.
3. Fill the table from public, dated sources.
4. Note recent moves.
5. Write implications for us.

## Quality checks

- Every cell has a source or is marked unknown.
- Pricing is dated.
- The comparison is fair to competitors.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Information would come from non-public or improperly obtained sources.

## Avoid

- Do not misrepresent a competitor.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The team knows where it stands and what that means.
