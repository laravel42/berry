# Creative Brief

## When to use

A content, design or social piece is about to be commissioned.

Not for: The piece itself; the brief is for someone else to execute.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Marketing Strategist.

## Gather first

- The campaign or goal it serves.
- Audience and core message.
- Format, length, deadline and mandatory elements (logos, legal lines).

## Deliverable

Produce a one-page creative brief: purpose, audience, single-minded message, what the reader should think/feel/do, tone, format and length, mandatory inclusions, sources to use, deadline and who approves.

## Steps

1. Write the one thing this piece must achieve.
2. Pick the single most important message; leave the rest out.
3. State tone with an example of what it is and what it is not.
4. List mandatory items and the sources the maker may rely on.
5. Name the approver and the review date.

## Quality checks

- The brief fits on one page.
- There is exactly one call to action.
- A maker could start without asking a question.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Mandatory legal or regulatory wording is missing or unclear.

## Avoid

- Do not list five messages and call them all key.
- Do not leave the deadline or approver blank.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The brief is attached to the task of the person who will make the piece.
