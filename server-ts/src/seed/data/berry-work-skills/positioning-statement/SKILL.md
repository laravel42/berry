# Positioning Statement

## When to use

An offer is being launched, relaunched or explained inconsistently across materials.

Not for: Visual identity or logo work; that belongs to a designer.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Marketing Strategist.

## Gather first

- The audience profile.
- The offer and what it actually does or includes.
- The real alternatives the audience uses today, including doing nothing.

## Deliverable

Produce a positioning document: the positioning statement, the main alternatives and how the offer differs, a message hierarchy (one core message, three supporting points, proof for each) and phrases to avoid.

## Steps

1. List the alternatives the audience would choose instead.
2. Write what the offer does better, worse and differently, honestly.
3. Draft the statement: for whom, what it is, the main benefit, why it is believable.
4. Build the message hierarchy with a proof point for every claim.
5. Test each message against the question "so what?" for the audience.

## Quality checks

- Every claim has proof the workspace holds or is marked as needing proof.
- Comparisons with named competitors are factual and sourced.
- The core message fits in one sentence a customer would say.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The positioning makes a claim about price, results, safety or a competitor that needs approval.
- It changes a brand commitment already made publicly.

## Avoid

- Do not use superlatives you cannot prove ("best", "only", "fastest").
- Do not copy a competitor's wording.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Anyone writing about the offer can use the hierarchy and stay consistent.
