# Qualitative Notes Synthesis

## When to use

Qualitative material has been collected and needs making sense of.

Not for: Statistical claims from small samples.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- Notes or transcripts, anonymised where possible.
- The research question.

## Deliverable

Produce a synthesis: themes with how many sources support each, quotes per theme, contradictions, surprises, and implications, with the sample described.

## Steps

1. Read everything once before coding.
2. Code passages and group codes into themes.
3. Count sources per theme.
4. Pick quotes that represent, not only the vivid ones.
5. Note contradictions and implications.

## Quality checks

- Each theme cites sources.
- Quotes are anonymised.
- Sample limits are stated.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Material reveals risk of harm to a participant.

## Avoid

- Do not turn "some people said" into "users want".

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

Themes are traceable to the notes and honest about the sample.
