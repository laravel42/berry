# Research Brief

## When to use

Evidence has been gathered for a framed question.

Not for: Raw note dumps.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The research plan.
- Evaluated sources and notes.

## Deliverable

Produce a brief: the answer in the first paragraph with a confidence level, key findings each with citations, where sources disagree, what is unknown and what would settle it, and a source list.

## Steps

1. Write the answer first, with how confident you are and why.
2. Support it with three to six findings, each cited.
3. Show disagreements between sources fairly.
4. List unknowns and how to resolve them.
5. Add the full source list with dates.

## Quality checks

- Every claim has a citation.
- Estimates are labelled as estimates.
- Confidence is stated and justified.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The answer would drive a decision about health, safety, money or law.

## Avoid

- Do not pick the convenient source when sources conflict.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A decision-maker can act on the first paragraph and check the rest.
