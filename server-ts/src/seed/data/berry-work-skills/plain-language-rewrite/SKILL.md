# Plain Language Rewrite

## When to use

Readers are the public, customers, patients, students or staff outside the specialism.

Not for: Legal documents whose exact wording has force; summarise alongside, do not replace.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Editor.

## Gather first

- The original text.
- Who reads it and their reading context.

## Deliverable

Produce a plain-language version with the same meaning, a list of terms kept and explained, and notes on anything that could not be simplified without losing meaning.

## Steps

1. Identify the reader and what they must do after reading.
2. Lead with what matters to them.
3. Use short sentences, common words and active voice.
4. Explain necessary terms once.
5. Compare meaning line by line with the original.

## Quality checks

- Meaning is preserved.
- Sentences average under about twenty words.
- Instructions are in the order the reader does them.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The text has legal, medical or safety force.

## Avoid

- Do not drop conditions or exceptions to make it shorter.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reader outside the field understands it the first time.
