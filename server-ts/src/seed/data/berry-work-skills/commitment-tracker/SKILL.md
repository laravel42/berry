# Commitment Tracker

## When to use

Commitments are scattered across meetings, messages and tasks.

Not for: Fulfilling the commitments.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Executive Assistant.

## Gather first

- Tasks, comments and threads a person shares.

## Deliverable

Produce a tracker: commitment, to whom, source, due date, status, and a list of items due in the next seven days.

## Steps

1. Scan sources for promises ("I will", "we will send", "by Friday").
2. Record each with source and date.
3. Ask the person to confirm unclear ones.
4. Sort by due date.
5. Highlight the next seven days.

## Quality checks

- Every item links to its source.
- Dates are explicit.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A commitment is overdue to an external party.

## Avoid

- Do not share one person's commitments with others without permission.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person can see every open promise and its date.
