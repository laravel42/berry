# Transcreation

## When to use

Marketing or creative text depends on wordplay, rhythm or cultural reference.

Not for: Contracts, instructions or anything where exact meaning matters more than effect.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Translator.

## Gather first

- Source copy and its brief (what it must make people feel and do).
- Target market.
- Brand voice.

## Deliverable

Produce two or three options per line with a back-translation, what each keeps and loses, and a recommendation.

## Steps

1. Restate the intent of the source line.
2. Create options that achieve the same effect locally.
3. Back-translate each.
4. Check for unintended meanings and sensitivities.
5. Recommend one and say why.

## Quality checks

- Back-translations are honest.
- No option carries an unintended meaning.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- A line carries legal claims or regulated wording.

## Avoid

- Do not keep a pun that does not work in the target language.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The local version lands the way the original does.
