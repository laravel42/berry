# Proposal and Grant Writing

## When to use

There is a request for proposals, a tender, a grant call or a prospect who asked for a written proposal.

Not for: Pricing decisions; prices come from a person or approved price list.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Content Writer.

## Gather first

- The call, tender or request, including evaluation criteria and limits.
- Facts about the organisation: track record, team, budget.
- Approved prices or budget figures.

## Deliverable

Produce a proposal draft that follows the requested structure, a compliance table mapping every requirement and criterion to the section that answers it, and a list of facts a person must confirm.

## Steps

1. Extract every requirement, criterion, word limit and deadline into a table.
2. Outline the response in the exact order the funder or buyer asks.
3. Answer each criterion with evidence, outcomes and how they will be measured.
4. Fill the budget only from approved figures; leave gaps marked.
5. Check every limit and every requirement in the table is met.

## Quality checks

- Every requirement maps to a section.
- Word and page limits are respected.
- Outcomes are specific and measurable.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The proposal commits the organisation to deliverables, dates or money not approved.
- Eligibility is in doubt.

## Avoid

- Do not overstate track record or invent partners.
- Do not submit or send; a person does.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A reviewer can see at a glance that every criterion is answered.
