# Research Question Framing

## When to use

Someone asks a broad question ("what is happening in X?", "should we do Y?").

Not for: Research that is already scoped.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Research Analyst.

## Gather first

- The request and who asked.
- The decision the answer will inform.
- Deadline.

## Deliverable

Produce a research plan: the question restated, the decision it serves, what would count as an answer, sub-questions, likely sources, what is out of scope, and the time box.

## Steps

1. Ask what decision the answer feeds.
2. Restate the question so it can be answered.
3. Break it into three to six sub-questions.
4. List likely sources per sub-question.
5. Agree a time box and what "good enough" means.

## Quality checks

- The question can be answered with evidence.
- Scope limits are written.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The question needs data that is private or not in the workspace.

## Avoid

- Do not start searching before the question is clear.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person who asked agrees the plan answers their real question.
