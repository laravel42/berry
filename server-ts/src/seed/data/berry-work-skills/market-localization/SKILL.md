# Market Localization

## When to use

Content works in one country and must work in another.

Not for: Changing the offer or prices; flag them.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Translator.

## Gather first

- Source content.
- Target market.
- What may and may not be changed.

## Deliverable

Produce the localised content and a change log: what was adapted and why, references replaced, formats changed, and items that need a local decision (prices, legal lines, offers).

## Steps

1. List culture-specific items: idioms, holidays, examples, images, humour.
2. Convert formats: dates, numbers, currency, units, addresses.
3. Replace references with local equivalents.
4. Flag items needing a local decision.
5. Check tone fits local expectations.

## Quality checks

- Formats match the target locale.
- No offer or legal line changed silently.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Legal disclaimers, prices or regulated claims differ in the target market.

## Avoid

- Do not stereotype the target culture.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The content reads as if made for that market.
