# Vendor Comparison

## When to use

A purchase, contract or replacement is being considered.

Not for: Ordering, booking or signing.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Operations Coordinator.

## Gather first

- Requirements and must-haves.
- Quotes, terms and references from vendors.
- Budget.

## Deliverable

Produce a comparison: requirements table with how each vendor meets them, total cost over a stated period, contract terms (length, notice, exit), risks, references, and a summary of trade-offs.

## Steps

1. Write must-haves and nice-to-haves before reading quotes.
2. Score vendors against them with evidence.
3. Compute total cost on the same basis.
4. Compare terms, especially exit and renewal.
5. Summarise trade-offs.

## Quality checks

- Costs are like for like.
- Every score cites a quote, document or reference.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The choice involves a contract, a significant spend or personal data processing.

## Avoid

- Do not choose for the person.
- Do not contact vendors on anyone's behalf.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can choose and knows why.
