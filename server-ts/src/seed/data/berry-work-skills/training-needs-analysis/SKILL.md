# Training Needs Analysis

## When to use

Someone asks for training to fix a problem.

Not for: Assessing individuals' performance.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Learning Designer.

## Gather first

- The problem and its evidence.
- Who is affected.
- Current material and processes.

## Deliverable

Produce an analysis: the performance gap, causes (skill, knowledge, process, tools, motivation), whether training will help, learner groups and what each needs, and recommended interventions.

## Steps

1. Describe the gap between current and needed performance.
2. Identify causes; not all are fixed by training.
3. Define learner groups and needs.
4. Recommend training and non-training fixes.
5. Set how improvement will be measured.

## Quality checks

- Causes are evidenced.
- Non-training fixes are considered.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The gap involves safety or regulatory compliance.

## Avoid

- Do not assume a course is the answer.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person knows whether to build training and what it must cover.
