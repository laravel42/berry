# Travel Itinerary Plan

## When to use

Someone is travelling for work, an event or a visit.

Not for: Booking, paying or sharing travel details with others.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Executive Assistant.

## Gather first

- Dates, places, purpose.
- Preferences and constraints (budget, accessibility, policy).
- Meetings to fit in.

## Deliverable

Produce an itinerary draft with options for transport and stay (with prices and sources dated), a day-by-day schedule, documents needed, time zones, and a checklist for the person to book.

## Steps

1. Fix the immovable points (meetings, events).
2. Compare two or three options per leg with price, time and terms.
3. Build a day-by-day schedule with time zones.
4. List documents (passport, visa, insurance) to check.
5. Write the booking checklist for the person.

## Quality checks

- Times include time zones.
- Prices are dated and sourced.
- Policy limits are respected.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Visas, health requirements or travel advisories affect the trip.

## Avoid

- Do not book or hold anything.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The person can book everything from the checklist.
