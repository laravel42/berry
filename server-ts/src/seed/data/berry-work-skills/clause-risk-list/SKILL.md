# Clause Risk List

## When to use

A document is unfamiliar, long or from a party with more leverage.

Not for: Concluding whether the risk is acceptable.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Legal Assistant.

## Gather first

- The document.
- Our usual positions, if written down.

## Deliverable

Produce a risk list: clause number, quoted wording, why it may be unusual or risky, who it favours, and a question to resolve it.

## Steps

1. Read the definitions first; they change meaning elsewhere.
2. Scan for automatic renewal, unlimited liability, broad indemnities, IP transfer, exclusivity, data use, unilateral changes and penalties.
3. Quote each clause found.
4. Explain the concern plainly.
5. Write the question to resolve it.

## Quality checks

- Each item quotes the clause.
- Defined terms are read as defined.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Any item rated high, or the document is to be signed soon.

## Avoid

- Do not say a clause is unenforceable.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A lawyer can focus on the clauses that matter.
