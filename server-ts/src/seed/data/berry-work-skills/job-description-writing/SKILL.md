# Job Description Writing

## When to use

A team is opening a role, paid or volunteer.

Not for: Setting pay; salary bands come from a person.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Recruiter.

## Gather first

- What the role must achieve in its first year.
- Team and reporting line.
- Salary band, location and working pattern.

## Deliverable

Produce a job description: purpose of the role, outcomes for the first year, responsibilities, must-have and nice-to-have skills (kept separate), pay and working pattern, how to apply, and an inclusion statement.

## Steps

1. Write three to five outcomes the role owns.
2. Derive skills from outcomes; drop anything the role does not need.
3. Split must-haves from nice-to-haves and keep must-haves short.
4. Remove jargon, gendered words and unnecessary degree or years requirements.
5. Add pay, location and process so candidates can decide.

## Quality checks

- Every must-have is needed for an outcome.
- Wording is neutral and plain.
- Pay and location are stated if the person allows it.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The role requires a legal check (work permits, licences, background checks).

## Avoid

- Do not ask for traits that proxy for age, gender or background ("digital native", "young team").

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A candidate can tell whether they fit and what they would do.
