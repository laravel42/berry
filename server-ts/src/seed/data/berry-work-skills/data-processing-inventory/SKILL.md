# Personal Data Inventory

## When to use

A privacy review, audit or new regulation requires it, or nobody knows.

Not for: Accessing the data itself.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Legal Assistant.

## Gather first

- Systems, forms and processes that handle personal data.
- Owners who can answer questions.

## Deliverable

Produce an inventory table: process, data categories, people concerned, purpose, legal basis to confirm, where stored, who has access, shared with whom, retention, and owner.

## Steps

1. List processes that touch personal data.
2. For each, record categories, purpose and storage.
3. Note sharing with third parties.
4. Record retention or mark it missing.
5. Flag sensitive categories.

## Quality checks

- Every row has an owner.
- Sensitive categories are flagged.
- Unknowns are marked, not guessed.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- Sensitive data has no clear purpose or retention.

## Avoid

- Do not copy personal data into the inventory; describe categories.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The organisation can answer "what personal data do we hold and why?".
