# Candidate Message Drafting

## When to use

A person has decided the next step for a candidate.

Not for: Deciding the outcome.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Recruiter.

## Gather first

- The decision made by a person.
- Stage and dates.
- Any feedback approved for sharing.

## Deliverable

Produce a message draft that is clear, warm and specific, states the decision or next step, practical details, and who to contact.

## Steps

1. Confirm the decision is recorded by a person.
2. State the decision or next step in the first two lines.
3. Give practical details: date, place or link, duration, who they will meet.
4. For rejections, thank them and share only approved feedback.
5. Offer adjustments for interviews.

## Quality checks

- The draft matches the recorded decision.
- No reason is given that touches a protected trait.
- Dates and times include a time zone.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The draft is an offer, a rejection after final stage, or anything involving pay.

## Avoid

- Do not leave candidates guessing about timing.
- Do not send; a person does.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can send the message as it is.
