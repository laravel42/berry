# Outreach Sequence Drafting

## When to use

An account brief exists and a person wants to start a conversation.

Not for: Bulk unsolicited mailing; drafts are for a person to send one by one.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Sales Specialist.

## Gather first

- Account brief.
- The offer and an approved reason to reach out.
- Channel (email, professional network, letter).

## Deliverable

Produce a sequence of up to three messages with timing, each short, specific to the account, with one ask, plus notes on what to check before sending.

## Steps

1. Open with something specific and true about the account.
2. Say in one line what you can help with and why now.
3. Ask for one small next step.
4. Write follow-ups that add something new, not just a reminder.
5. List anything the sender must verify.

## Quality checks

- Each message is under about 120 words.
- Nothing claims a relationship or referral that does not exist.
- Opt-out wording is included where local rules require it.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- The message would offer a discount, term or commitment not approved.

## Avoid

- Do not use fake personalisation or false familiarity.
- Do not send; a person does.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

A person can send the first message after a two-minute review.
