# Case Study Writing

## When to use

There is a real story with a client, beneficiary or project willing to be featured.

Not for: Anonymous or invented stories presented as real.

Load this skill when this exact piece of work is needed, not merely because the task belongs to the Content Writer.

## Gather first

- Interview notes or call transcript.
- Results with their source.
- Written consent and any approval requirements from the subject.

## Deliverable

Produce a case study: headline result, the situation before, what was done, the results with sources, approved quotes, and a list of items the subject must approve.

## Steps

1. Confirm consent exists in the workspace; if not, stop and ask.
2. Lead with the result in the subject's terms.
3. Tell before, during and after in that order.
4. Use quotes only from the notes, word for word or marked as paraphrase.
5. List every quote and figure for the subject to approve.

## Quality checks

- Every quote traces to notes.
- Results have a date and a source.
- Confidential details are removed or approved.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- There is no record of consent.
- The story touches health, finance or legal outcomes of a person.

## Avoid

- Do not polish quotes into things nobody said.
- Do not reveal names of people who did not agree.

## Ground rules

- Work from what the workspace holds: the task, its comments and files, the project's resources and linked goals. When something essential is missing, ask in a comment instead of guessing.
- Write the deliverable as a file on the task (or in the project, when the task has one), named for what it is.
- Keep every source next to the claim it supports, and label every assumption as one.
- You draft; a person sends, publishes, posts, pays, signs, books and decides.
- Use personal data only as far as the task needs it, and never repeat it elsewhere.

## Hand back

End with a short note on the task: what you produced and where it is, what you checked, what you assumed, and what a person must decide or verify before it is used.

## Done when

The draft is ready for the subject's approval.
